\begin{abstract}
The evolution of networks has increased their complexity and driven a shift toward
distributed architectures, from enterprise networks to the Internet of Things and
cyber-physical systems. While this shift opens new landscapes and opportunities, it also
enlarges the attack surface, calling for adaptive distributed intrusion detection that
keeps working while its own nodes fail. Federated learning (FL) has been widely adopted
to train intrusion detectors across distributed networks, coordinating a shared model
through a server; gossip learning (GL), far less explored, lets peers exchange models
directly, without a server. This research presents ReSIDS, which combines both: it fuses
per-attack gradient-boosted specialists by k-of-n voting and switches between FL and GL
at runtime. A federated failure detector monitors the nodes and commands each switch:
the system moves to GL when nodes fail and returns to FL once they recover. The
architecture is not tied to one kind of network: applying it to another requires only
retraining the specialists. Because attacks are rare in real traffic, individual alerts
are dominated by false alarms; ReSIDS therefore aggregates them over windows, with
thresholds calibrated on held-out data. On public IEC 61850 substation traffic (ERENO),
detection is unchanged as membership shrinks from 14 to 3 nodes (F1-score 95.98\%), and
windowed alarms flag 97.9\% of attack windows at a 0.14\% false-alarm rate. On IoT
(CICIoT2023) and enterprise (corrected CICIDS2017) traffic, [RESULT]. Runtime switching
makes the learning topology an operational choice rather than a design-time commitment.
\end{abstract}
