\begin{abstract}
The evolution of networks has increased their complexity and driven a shift toward
distributed cyber and cyber-physical systems, from enterprise networks to the Internet of
Things and critical infrastructure. This shift enlarges the attack surface, calling for
resilient, adaptive intrusion detection that keeps working while its own nodes fail.
In federated learning (FL), a server aggregates detectors trained at the nodes; in gossip
learning (GL), far less explored, nodes exchange them directly. This research presents ReSIDS,
an intrusion detection system that combines both: it fuses per-attack gradient-boosted
specialists by OR, keeps every specialist it receives, and switches its learning mode at
runtime. A federated failure detector, Disaster-FD, supplies a trust level from which each
node switches by itself: to FL while the aggregator answers, to GL when it is lost, and to
local learning when cut off. Under two conditions, a switching node provably holds every
specialist that fixed FL or GL would give it. Porting ReSIDS requires only a domain profile
and retrained specialists. On IEC~61850 substation (ERENO), enterprise (improved CICIDS2017),
gas-pipeline control (Morris-4) and IoT (CICIoT2023) traffic, replayed on two Disaster-FD runs
that meet both conditions, fixed FL and GL fell up to 82.0 and 48.1 points behind switching in
protecting nodes against new attacks; retention kept every attack type as nodes were lost.
At 0.1\,\% prevalence, at least 86\,\% of per-sample alerts were false in every domain; ReSIDS
therefore counts alerts per window, network-wide and per specialist. Adaptive switching turns
the learning topology into an operational choice.
\end{abstract}
