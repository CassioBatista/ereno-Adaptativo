\paragraph{When switching matters}
The recorded runs fix the length of each outage; constructed scenarios vary it, to measure what
each choice of mode costs when the length of an outage is not known in advance. On the ring of 14
nodes with every link up, so that C1 and C2 hold, the aggregator is lost for $D$ rounds, from 0 to
60, as the block of nodes 3, 4, 11 and 12 arrives, and the specialists held by each node are
propagated offline by the rules of the pipeline, which reproduces the holdings of every node in
every round of the 24 runs above exactly. A mode fixed at design time is wrong in one regime or the
other: fixed FL leaves up to 80.6 points more node-rounds unprotected than switching after a long
outage (75.8 in ERENO), and fixed GL up to 24.2 points more when the aggregator is never lost (12.3
in CICIDS2017), whereas switching is never behind either, as Proposition~\ref{prop:dominance}
guarantees. The two fixed modes cross at the time gossip takes to spread a specialist over the
ring, 8 rounds in CICIDS2017 and 15 in ERENO: for shorter outages fixed FL is the better fixed
mode, for longer ones fixed GL. Even against the fixed mode that would be chosen knowing the length
of the outage, switching loses nothing, and gains up to 2.5 points near the crossover. When, as in
the recorded runs, a second block arrives as the aggregator returns, the better mode changes within
the scenario and no fixed mode is right: in CICIDS2017, switching then gains up to 26.2 points over
the better fixed mode, with outages of 20 to 40 rounds, and fixed GL falls up to 27.7 points
behind. In ERENO, the types of the second block also have their other specialist on nodes present
from the start, and the second block changes nothing. In Morris-4 and CICIoT2023, the specialists
already present detect the block that arrives during the outage, so fixed FL loses nothing, and
the mode matters only against fixed GL, which falls up to 19.6 and 18.9 points behind with two
blocks. The boosters of seeds 43 to 46 give the same figures, except, with two blocks, the largest
gain in CICIDS2017 (26.2 to 26.4 points) and the largest lag of fixed GL in CICIDS2017 (27.7 to
29.2) and CICIoT2023 (18.9 to 20.0). With rounds of 60\,s, the crossover corresponds to outages of 8
to 15\,min; for another period or overlay, it scales with the number of gossip rounds needed to
cross the overlay.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- (b2) Discussion, before the
% closing paragraph ("Across the four domains, three results held ...")
Four findings concern the evaluation of distributed intrusion detection rather than ReSIDS. First,
detectors evaluated at the prevalence of their test split are not usable sample by sample in
operation: at a prevalence of 0.1\,\%, at least 86\,\% of the per-sample alerts are false in all
four domains, and the centralized model, trained on every row, does not escape this in three of
them (Section~\ref{sec:res-windows}). Second, a high recall can hide a detector that flags
whatever it has not seen: in Morris-4, 29.3\,\% of the benign test rows have a feature vector absent
from training, both partitions flag 99.2\,\% of them, and their recall of 99.8\,\% is no evidence of
detection (Section~\ref{sec:res-partition}). Third, labels assigned per capture session rather than
per attack produce specialists that cannot separate their type from benign traffic: in CICIoT2023
they raise the false-positive rate of the attack-based partition to 28.6\,\%, against 0.68\,\% under
IID, and the coverage rule, decided on training rows alone, brings it to 0.61\,\% at a recall of
99.5\,\% by dropping them (Section~\ref{sec:res-nodeloss}). Fourth, alarming per window trades the
isolated attacks for few false alarms: a window with a single attack flow alarms in 1 of 106 cases
in CICIDS2017, and attacks thinned to a prevalence of 0.03\,\% reach the operator in 24.1\,\% of
their samples, although the specialists flag 99.5\,\% of them (Section~\ref{sec:res-windows}). Each
of these would stay hidden in an evaluation that reports only per-sample metrics at the prevalence
of an attack-rich test split.
