\paragraph{When switching matters}
The recorded runs fix the length of each outage; constructed scenarios vary it, to measure what
each choice of mode costs when the length of an outage is not known in advance. On the ring of 14
nodes with every link up, so that C1 and C2 hold, the aggregator is lost for $D$ rounds, from 0 to
60, as the block of nodes 3, 4, 11 and 12 arrives, and the specialists held by each node are
propagated offline by the rules of the pipeline, which reproduces the holdings of every node in
every round of the 24 runs above exactly. A mode fixed at design time is wrong in one regime or the
other: fixed FL leaves up to 80.6 points more node-rounds unprotected than switching after a long
outage (75.8 in ERENO), and fixed GL up to 24.2 points more when the aggregator is never lost (12.3
in CICIDS2017), whereas switching is never behind either, as Proposition~\ref{prop:dominance}
guarantees. The two fixed modes cross at the time gossip takes to spread a specialist over the
ring, 8 rounds in CICIDS2017 and 15 in ERENO: for shorter outages fixed FL is the better fixed
mode, for longer ones fixed GL. Even against the fixed mode that would be chosen knowing the length
of the outage, switching loses nothing, and gains up to 2.5 points near the crossover. When, as in
the recorded runs, a second block arrives as the aggregator returns, the better mode changes within
the scenario and no fixed mode is right: in CICIDS2017, switching then gains up to 26.2 points over
the better fixed mode, with outages of 20 to 40 rounds, and fixed GL falls up to 27.7 points
behind. In ERENO, the types of the second block also have their other specialist on nodes present
from the start, and the second block changes nothing. In Morris-4 and CICIoT2023, the specialists
already present detect the block that arrives during the outage, so fixed FL loses nothing, and
the mode matters only against fixed GL, which falls up to 19.6 and 18.9 points behind with two
blocks. The boosters of seeds 43 to 46 give the same figures, except, with two blocks, the largest
gain in CICIDS2017 (26.2 to 26.4 points) and the largest lag of fixed GL in CICIDS2017 (27.7 to
29.2) and CICIoT2023 (18.9 to 20.0). With rounds of 60\,s, the crossover corresponds to outages of 8
to 15\,min; for another period or overlay, it scales with the number of gossip rounds needed to
cross the overlay.

\subsection{Findings for the evaluation of distributed intrusion detection}
\label{sec:res-findings}
Four results of Sections~\ref{sec:res-partition} to~\ref{sec:res-windows} concern how distributed
intrusion detection is evaluated rather than ReSIDS itself. Each would stay hidden in an
evaluation that reports only per-sample metrics at the prevalence of an attack-rich test split,
and each is exposed by a quantity that such an evaluation can add without changing the detector,
from its per-sample decisions, its training rows and the timestamps of its test traffic
(Table~\ref{tab:findings}).

The evaluation also yields four findings that per-sample metrics at the prevalence of an
attack-rich test split hide: most alerts are false at operational prevalence, benign traffic
absent from training passes for detection, labels per capture session make imprecise
detectors, and window alarms miss isolated attacks; each is exposed by a quantity that any
evaluation can report (Table~\ref{tab:findings}).

For the operator who validates the alarms, this is the trade-off that matters: at a prevalence
of 0.1\,\%, at least 86 of every 100 per-sample alerts are false in every domain, the condition
that leads analysts to alarm burnout~\cite{alahmadi2022soc}, whereas in the three domains with
ordered traffic a window alarms falsely in 0.14 to 2.9\,\% of the attack-free windows; what the
windows cost the operator is the isolated attacks.

These properties make the control plane trustworthy in the sense of dependable engineering: its
behaviour is guaranteed under stated conditions (Proposition~\ref{prop:dominance}) rather than
only observed on average, a faulty monitor can degrade only its own node, and every switch can
be re-derived from the evidence that caused it (Section~\ref{sec:impl}).

% ---------------------------------------------------------------- (c4) Section 3.5, "Transport and
% security", last sentence
Byzantine monitors, which could vouch for a compromised aggregator, are outside the scope of
this work, as are attacks on the learning itself, such as poisoned training rows or a
specialist crafted to flag benign traffic. Under OR fusion, a specialist crafted to miss an
attack cannot hide it from another specialist that flags it, but one that flags benign traffic
raises false alarms on every node that holds it, and retention keeps it there; the coverage rule
of Section~\ref{sec:res-nodeloss}, applied to the specialists a node receives, would screen out
one that flags benign training rows, but not one crafted to pass that screen.
