\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This research presents ReSIDS~v2, an intrusion detection system for networks whose own nodes may fail. Each node switches by itself between local, gossip and federated learning on the trust level that the federated failure detector Disaster-FD supplies, detects with attack specialists fused by OR, retains every specialist it receives, and raises its alarms per window. The core is the same in every domain; only a domain profile and the specialists change.

Evaluated on four domains, an IEC~61850 substation, an enterprise network, a gas-pipeline control system and an IoT network, and on two runs recorded by Disaster-FD, ReSIDS shows that the decision to switch can be left to the nodes. Federating while the aggregator answers and gossiping when it is lost, it had the smallest share of nodes without protection against new attacks in every combination of domain, run and attack block in which the configurations differ, and in no round did it protect fewer nodes than fixed federated or fixed gossip learning, which fell up to 82.0 and 48.1 points behind. Retention kept every attack type as nodes were lost, and the specialists of a region left it before the region was cut off. Counting alerts per window and per specialist turned per-sample alerts, at least 86\,\% of them false at a prevalence of 0.1\,\%, into alarms on 70.2 to 99.3\,\% of the attack windows, with false alarms in 0.14 to 2.9\,\% of the attack-free ones, for attacks that come in volume.

The results separate what belongs to ReSIDS from what belongs to the data. Switching, retention and the alarm rule held in every domain; the false positives of the specialists and the length of the window followed the traffic of each domain, and are set at deployment from its own data.

The next step is to close the loop between detection and failure handling: the agent reading the trust level from a running Disaster-FD, and ReSIDS reporting intrusions back to it, so that a node found compromised can be isolated and readmitted, with trust in the monitors that resists Byzantine behaviour. On the detection side, the OR fusion can give way to a counselors network~\cite{quincozes2021counselors}, in which a node settles the conflicting decisions of its specialists by consulting other nodes, to curb the false positives of the specialists that cannot separate their types from benign traffic without the veto of a fixed quorum; the specialists that the nodes keep after an exchange can be chosen by the coverage rule of Section~\ref{sec:res-nodeloss}, and the isolated attacks that stay below the window thresholds call for an alarm path of their own.
